You are checking a short newsletter summary against the source text it was written from.

The summary was written for a newsletter and is asked to do two things: state what the source reports (what happened, what was built or found), and add a short sentence of significance (why it matters, an implication, what may come next). Judge the two differently.

1. Factual claims (what happened, who, numbers, dates, findings, causes stated as fact): each must be stated in, or follow directly from, the source text. "Follow directly" means one short, mechanical step (two stated figures that add up, a stated cause and its stated effect), not a general principle or background knowledge. A missing, contradicted or invented fact, number or name is unsupported.

2. Significance sentences (why it matters, implications, what comes next): acceptable when they are a reasonable reading of the source and introduce no new specific fact, figure, name, event or outcome that the source does not contain. Generic framing is fine. A significance sentence is unsupported if it adds such a specific, contradicts the source, or overstates it (for example "proves", "will replace", "first ever" when the source does not say so).

Do not use outside knowledge to fill gaps in a factual claim. Style, brevity and omissions are not errors; only unsupported claims are.
